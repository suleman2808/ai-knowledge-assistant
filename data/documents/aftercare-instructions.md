# Aftercare Instructions

## After a Filling

A white composite filling sets hard immediately, so you may eat as soon as
you feel comfortable.

If you have had local anaesthetic, avoid chewing until the numbness has
fully worn off, usually two to four hours. Biting a numb lip or cheek is
the most common injury after a filling, and it is particularly easy for
children to do without noticing.

Mild sensitivity to cold for a few days is normal, especially with a deep
filling. Sensitivity that worsens after a week, or pain on biting, should
be reported to us — the filling may need adjusting, which takes minutes.

## After a Scale and Polish

Gums may feel tender and bleed slightly for a day or two, particularly if
there was significant tartar. This settles.

Rinse with warm salt water twice daily for three days. Brush gently but do
not stop brushing; gums recover faster when kept clean.

If you had a deep cleaning under anaesthetic, avoid very hot food and drink
until the numbness passes, and expect sensitivity to cold for up to two
weeks.

## After an Extraction

The first 24 hours matter most.

**Do:**
- Bite on the gauze pad for 30 minutes after leaving.
- Rest for the remainder of the day, keeping your head slightly elevated.
- Apply a cold compress to the cheek, 20 minutes on and 20 minutes off, for
  the first 6 hours.
- Eat soft, cool food: yoghurt, soup that is not hot, scrambled egg.
- Take pain relief before the anaesthetic wears off rather than waiting.

**Do not:**
- Rinse, spit or use mouthwash for 24 hours. This dislodges the clot.
- Drink through a straw for 5 days. The suction is the usual cause of dry
  socket.
- Smoke for at least 72 hours. Smoking is the single largest risk factor
  for dry socket and roughly triples the likelihood.
- Drink alcohol for 24 hours, or while taking prescribed painkillers.
- Exercise strenuously for 48 hours.

From the second day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals.

**Dry socket** typically appears three to four days afterwards as a
throbbing ache spreading to the ear, often with a bad taste, and pain worse
than the day of the extraction. It is treatable in a single short visit.
Call us — do not wait it out.

## After a Root Canal

The tooth will feel tender to bite on for several days, sometimes up to a
week. This is inflammation of the ligament around the tooth, not failure of
the treatment.

Chew on the other side until the permanent restoration is placed. A tooth
that has had a root canal is brittle and can fracture, which is why we
recommend a crown for back teeth. A fractured root-treated tooth usually
cannot be saved.

Contact us if swelling develops, if the temporary filling comes out, or if
pain increases rather than gradually settling.

## After a Crown or Veneer

**With a temporary crown:** avoid sticky and hard food, and floss by
pulling the thread out sideways rather than lifting it up, which can
dislodge the temporary. If it comes off, keep it and call us; leaving the
prepared tooth uncovered lets it move and sensitise.

**After the permanent crown is fitted:** avoid hard food for 24 hours.
Cold sensitivity for a week or two is common.

If the bite feels high, or you are aware of touching that tooth first when
you close, contact us. A crown that is fractionally too tall causes
persistent ache and is corrected in a few minutes.

## After Whitening

Avoid anything that would stain a white shirt for 48 hours: coffee, tea,
red wine, cola, curry, tomato sauce, berries, beetroot, soy sauce. Avoid
tobacco entirely during this period.

Sharp, brief "zingers" for one to two days are normal and are not damaging
the tooth. Sensitive toothpaste helps; use it for a week beforehand if you
know you are sensitive.

Results typically last one to three years depending on diet and smoking.

## After Implant Surgery

Take all prescribed medication, including antibiotics, to completion.

Do not disturb the surgical site with your tongue or fingers. Some swelling
and bruising is expected, peaking at 48 to 72 hours and then improving. Use
a cold compress for the first day and warm compresses after that.

Sleep with your head elevated for the first two nights.

Resume brushing other teeth immediately, avoiding the surgical site until
your review appointment. Use the prescribed chlorhexidine rinse from the
day after surgery, not on the day itself.

Do not smoke. Implant failure rates are substantially higher in smokers,
and the first two weeks are the most critical period.

## When to Contact Us After Any Treatment

Call us if you experience:

- Pain that increases after the third day rather than settling
- Swelling that worsens after 72 hours
- Fever
- Bleeding that does not stop with 20 minutes of firm pressure
- A reaction to prescribed medication
- Numbness persisting more than 8 hours after treatment

None of these are common. All of them are worth a phone call.
