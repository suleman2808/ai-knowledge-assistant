# Urgent Situations

A laboratory is not an emergency service. This page is about knowing when
not to wait for us.

## Go to an Emergency Department, Not to Us

Call 911 or attend an emergency department for:

- Chest pain, pressure or tightness
- Difficulty breathing
- Sudden weakness or numbness on one side, drooping face, slurred speech
- Severe bleeding that does not stop with pressure
- Sudden severe abdominal pain
- Fainting, confusion, or a seizure
- A fever above 103°F with a rash that does not fade under pressure
- Any sudden severe symptom you have not had before

None of these are waited on for a test result. A blood test tells you
about the past few hours; an emergency department can act now.

The nearest emergency departments are OHSU Hospital, Providence Portland
Medical Center and Legacy Good Samaritan.

## Critical Results

Some results need attention within hours. Our pathologist reviews every
critical value immediately and we telephone the referring doctor. Where
no doctor is named, we telephone you.

Values that trigger a call include a haemoglobin below 7 g/dL, a
potassium below 2.5 or above 6.5 mmol/L, a blood glucose below 50 or
above 450 mg/dL, a platelet count below 20,000, a positive blood culture,
and an INR above 5.

**If we call you about a critical result, go to an emergency department
or contact a doctor the same day.** We will say which. We cannot tell you
what it means or what will be done about it — that is a doctor's
judgement — but we would not call if waiting were safe.

We call at any hour, including nights and weekends. If we cannot reach
you, we keep trying and we contact the doctor named on the request.

## Urgent Testing

Some tests can be prioritised when a doctor needs an answer today.

Tell reception the request is urgent and we will say honestly whether it
changes anything. Some tests cannot be hurried: a culture needs its
incubation period, and no amount of urgency grows bacteria faster.

Urgent processing is $40 on top of the test price, and is only available
for same-day tests collected before 2:00 PM.

## After a Blood Draw

Contact us on (503) 555-0142 if:

- Bleeding has not stopped after ten minutes of firm pressure
- The bruise is spreading rather than fading after a day
- The arm becomes swollen, hot or increasingly painful
- There is numbness, tingling or weakness in the hand

These are uncommon. The last one is the one to call about promptly.

Attend an emergency department instead if the arm becomes rapidly
swollen and painful, or if there are signs of a serious reaction —
difficulty breathing, swelling of the face or throat, widespread rash.

## Feeling Faint During Collection

Common, harmless, and nothing to apologise for. Tell the phlebotomist,
who will stop and lay you flat. It passes faster lying down than sitting.

If you have fainted during a blood draw before, say so when you arrive.
We will collect you lying down from the start. It is easier for everyone
than dealing with it afterwards.

## Needlestick and Spills

If you are injured by a needle on our premises, tell a member of staff
immediately. We have a documented protocol, we will arrange immediate
assessment, and there is no charge.

## Out of Hours

We have no out-of-hours service. Outside collection hours, telephone
lines carry a recorded message with emergency numbers.

Results released overnight are sent by email and to the patient portal at
the time they are authorised, which may be late in the evening. Receiving
a report at 9:00 PM does not mean anything is wrong, and nobody is
expecting you to act on it that night unless we have telephoned you.
