# Reports and Results

## When Your Report Is Ready

Turnaround is listed against each test in the Test Catalogue. It is
counted from when the sample reaches the Hawthorne laboratory, not from
when it was taken.

"Same day" means by 8:00 PM for samples taken before 2:00 PM. A sample
taken at 5:00 PM is a next-day report, even for a same-day test.

Samples collected at Beaverton and Gresham travel to Hawthorne on the
11:00 AM and 4:00 PM courier runs, so a Gresham sample taken at 11:30 AM
begins processing after 4:00 PM.

If a test needs repeating — a clotted sample, an unexpected result that
warrants a re-run — the report is delayed and we telephone you to say so.
We would rather tell you it is late than send you something we do not
trust.

Reports are not released over the telephone. Staff answering the phone
cannot see enough of your record to identify you safely, and a result
read aloud to the wrong person cannot be taken back.

## Collecting a Printed Report

Bring photographic identification. If someone else is collecting on your
behalf, they need their own identification and a signed note from you
naming them. We will not hand a report to a family member without it, and
being a spouse or a parent of an adult patient makes no difference.

Reports for patients under 18 are released to a parent or legal guardian.

## Critical Results

Some results need a doctor's attention within hours, not days — a
dangerously low haemoglobin, a potassium level that affects the heart, a
positive blood culture.

When a result crosses a critical threshold, our pathologist reviews it
immediately, and we telephone the referring doctor. If no doctor is
named, we telephone you and tell you to go to an emergency department or
contact a doctor today.

This happens whatever the hour, including nights and weekends. It is why
we ask for a telephone number we can actually reach you on.

**A call from us about a critical result is not a diagnosis.** It means a
number is outside the range where waiting is safe. What it means for you
is a question for a doctor.

## Reference Ranges

Every report shows the reference range beside the result. A value outside
that range is flagged High or Low.

Ranges differ by age, sex, pregnancy, and sometimes by the method the
laboratory uses. A result from us is not directly comparable with one
from another laboratory using a different method, which is why your
doctor may ask you to repeat a test here rather than accept an outside
report.

Being slightly outside a reference range is common and often means
nothing at all. Ranges are defined so that 95% of healthy people fall
inside them, which means one healthy person in twenty falls outside by
definition.

## We Cannot Interpret Your Results

This is the question we are asked most, and the answer is always the
same.

Our staff — including the people who ran your test — cannot tell you what
your result means for you, whether it is serious, what might have caused
it, or what to do next. Not by telephone, not at the counter, not by
email.

This is not us being unhelpful. A result means something only alongside
your symptoms, your history, your medication and your examination, and we
have none of those. The same haemoglobin figure is unremarkable in one
person and needs investigating urgently in another.

Take your report to the doctor who requested it. If a test was arranged
without a doctor, any general practitioner can interpret it, and we will
gladly recommend one.

What we **can** tell you: whether a report is ready, what a test measures
in general terms, how a sample is collected, what the reference range on
your report is, and whether a result was flagged for a critical callback.

## Corrections and Repeat Testing

If a result is amended after release, you receive the corrected report
and so does the referring doctor, with the change marked. Amendments are
rare and always explained.

If you doubt a result, tell us. We will re-run the stored sample at no
charge where the sample is still viable, usually within seven days of
collection. Beyond that a fresh sample is needed and the normal price
applies.

## How Long We Keep Reports

Reports are kept for seven years, and until a patient who was a minor
reaches 25, whichever is later. Past reports stay available in the
patient portal for that period.

Samples themselves are kept for seven days and then destroyed, except
where a longer period is required for a specific test.
