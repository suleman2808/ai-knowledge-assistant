# Frequently Asked Questions

## How often should I have a check-up?

Every six months for most adults. Patients with healthy teeth, no fillings
and no gum disease can often move to annual check-ups, and we will tell you
if that applies to you. Patients with gum disease, a high decay rate or
dry mouth are usually seen every three to four months.

## Do you see nervous patients?

Yes, and a substantial proportion of our patients describe themselves as
nervous. Dr Samuel Reyes has specific training in treating dental phobia.

We offer longer appointments, stop signals agreed in advance, and
appointments where nothing is done except talking. There is no charge for
an introductory consultation of this kind. Many of our patients had not
seen a dentist for over a decade before registering with us.

## Does it hurt?

Most treatment is carried out under local anaesthetic and should not hurt.
The injection itself is the part patients dislike; we use topical numbing
gel first and give the anaesthetic slowly, which makes a real difference.

Tell us if you feel anything. Needing more anaesthetic is common and is not
a sign of anything being wrong.

## Can I bring someone with me?

Yes. A friend or family member is welcome in the treatment room, and many
patients find it helps.

## Do you treat children?

We see children from age three. Children under 16 must be accompanied by a
parent or guardian who remains in the building.

We do not provide general anaesthetic or complex paediatric surgery and
refer these cases to a paediatric specialist.

## Is whitening safe?

Professionally supervised whitening using carbamide or hydrogen peroxide is
safe and does not damage enamel. Temporary sensitivity is common and
resolves within a few days.

We do not recommend over-the-counter whitening kits bought online. The gel
concentration is often unregulated and poorly fitting trays cause chemical
burns to the gums, which we see several times a year.

## Why do I need X-rays?

Roughly a third of decay is invisible on examination because it sits
between teeth or beneath existing fillings. X-rays also show bone levels,
abscesses and impacted teeth.

We take bitewings every 18 to 24 months for most adults, more often for
patients at high risk of decay. Modern digital X-rays use around 80% less
radiation than film, and a bitewing pair is roughly equivalent to one day
of natural background radiation.

## I am pregnant. Can I be treated?

Yes, and you should be. Pregnancy commonly causes gum inflammation, and
untreated gum disease is associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes.

Routine examinations, cleanings and fillings are safe throughout pregnancy.
The second trimester is the most comfortable time for longer treatment.
Elective X-rays and non-urgent cosmetic work are deferred until after
delivery, though a shielded X-ray is safe where clinically necessary.

Tell us you are pregnant, and tell us again at each visit.

## What is the difference between a filling and a crown?

A filling replaces a small amount of lost tooth structure and is placed
directly in a single visit. A crown covers the whole tooth and is made in a
laboratory or milled in the practice.

The deciding factor is how much sound tooth remains. Once roughly half the
tooth is gone, a large filling tends to flex and eventually split the
tooth, and a crown is the more economical choice over time even though it
costs more up front.

## My gums bleed when I brush. Is that normal?

No. Bleeding gums are the most common early sign of gum disease and are
worth an appointment.

Healthy gums do not bleed. The usual instinct is to brush the area less,
which makes it worse; the correct response is to clean it more thoroughly,
along with professional treatment.

## How long do fillings last?

A composite filling typically lasts 7 to 10 years, though this varies
widely with size, position, diet and grinding. Small fillings in low-stress
areas often last far longer.

## Do you offer emergency appointments?

Yes. We hold same-day slots for urgent problems and operate an out-of-hours
line for registered patients on (503) 555-0143. See the Emergency and
Urgent Care document for what qualifies.

## Can I see the same dentist each time?

Yes, and we encourage it. Request your preferred dentist when booking.
Continuity is clinically useful, though it may mean waiting slightly longer
for an appointment.

## What happens if I have not been to a dentist in years?

You will not be lectured. We see this constantly and it is far more common
than people assume.

The first visit is an examination and a conversation. We produce a written
plan, usually in stages, prioritising anything painful or urgent first and
spreading the rest over a period that suits you financially.

## Do you accept walk-ins?

Not for routine care. For genuine emergencies, telephone first — we will
almost always find a way to see you, but calling ahead means the right
amount of time and the right clinician is available.
