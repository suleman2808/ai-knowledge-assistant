# Emergency and Urgent Care

## What Counts as a Dental Emergency

Contact us the same day for:

- Severe or worsening toothache, particularly if it disturbs sleep
- Facial swelling, or swelling of the gum, jaw or under the eye
- A knocked-out permanent tooth
- A tooth broken with bleeding or exposed nerve
- Uncontrolled bleeding after an extraction
- A dental abscess, or a bad taste with pain and swelling
- Trauma to the mouth following an accident

Call 911 or attend an emergency department instead if there is swelling
that affects breathing or swallowing, swelling spreading toward the eye or
neck, a fever above 101°F with facial swelling, or a suspected broken jaw.
These are medical emergencies and need a hospital, not a dental practice.

## What Can Usually Wait

The following are uncomfortable but not emergencies, and are normally seen
within a week:

- A lost filling or crown with no pain
- A chipped tooth with no sharp edge and no pain
- Mild sensitivity to hot or cold
- A broken or uncomfortable denture
- Food repeatedly trapped between teeth
- A loose orthodontic bracket

## Emergency Appointments During Opening Hours

Telephone as early in the day as possible. We reserve same-day slots for
urgent problems and allocate them by clinical need.

Reception will ask a short series of questions about your symptoms. This is
triage, not gatekeeping — it decides how urgently you are seen and how much
time to allow.

Emergency examination is $110, which includes an X-ray if needed. Treatment
is quoted separately once the problem is identified.

## Out of Hours

Existing patients can reach our on-call dentist outside opening hours on
**(503) 555-0143**. Calls are answered between 7:00 AM and 10:00 PM, seven
days a week, including holidays.

The on-call dentist provides advice and, where necessary, arranges to meet
you at the practice. An out-of-hours call-out is $250 in addition to the
cost of treatment.

Patients who are not registered with us are given advice free of charge and
directed to the Multnomah Dental Society emergency referral line on
(503) 555-0199.

## First Aid Before You Are Seen

**Knocked-out permanent tooth.** Handle it by the crown, never the root. If
it is dirty, rinse briefly in milk or saline — not water, and do not scrub.
Reinsert it into the socket if you can and bite gently on a clean cloth. If
you cannot, keep it in milk, or in the patient's own saliva. Get to us
within 30 minutes if at all possible. A tooth reimplanted within an hour
has a good chance of surviving; after two hours the outlook is poor.

**Knocked-out baby tooth.** Do not reinsert it. Doing so can damage the
developing adult tooth underneath. Contact us for advice.

**Toothache.** Rinse with warm salt water. Take paracetamol or ibuprofen at
the normal dose if you can take them safely. Do not place aspirin against
the gum — it burns the tissue. Do not apply heat to a swollen face.

**Broken tooth.** Rinse the mouth with warm water and apply a cold compress
to the outside of the cheek to limit swelling. Keep any fragments and bring
them with you.

**Bleeding after an extraction.** Bite firmly on clean gauze or a folded
handkerchief for 20 minutes without checking it. Repeat once if needed.
Avoid rinsing, spitting and hot drinks. If bleeding continues heavily after
two attempts, call us.

**Abscess.** Do not attempt to burst it. Rinse with warm salt water and
contact us the same day. An untreated dental abscess can spread and become
genuinely dangerous.
