# Preparation Instructions

What to do before a test, and what to do afterwards. Preparing wrongly is
the commonest reason a sample has to be taken twice.

## Fasting

Fasting means **no food and no drink except plain water**. Water is not
only allowed but encouraged: a dehydrated vein is harder to find and the
draw takes longer.

| Test | Fasting required |
| --- | --- |
| Lipid profile | 9–12 hours |
| Fasting blood glucose | 8 hours |
| Glucose tolerance test | 8 hours |
| Basic health check panel | 9–12 hours |
| Cardiac risk panel | 9–12 hours |
| Everything else | None |

Do not fast longer than 14 hours. Beyond that the body begins to break
down fat for energy and the lipid result is distorted in the other
direction.

**During the fast you may:** drink plain water, take prescribed
medication with water unless told otherwise, brush your teeth.

**During the fast you must not:** eat anything at all, drink tea, coffee
(including black), juice, milk or fizzy drinks, chew gum, smoke, or drink
alcohol.

Black coffee is the one people argue about. It alters lipid and glucose
readings. It counts as breaking the fast.

## Medication

**Do not stop any prescribed medication to prepare for a test unless the
doctor who prescribed it has told you to.** A missed dose is a real risk;
a slightly altered result usually is not.

Tell the phlebotomist what you take. Several things change results and
knowing about them is better than a repeat sample:

- **Biotin supplements** (often in hair, skin and nail products) interfere
  with thyroid and hormone assays. Stop 48 hours before, or tell us so we
  can note it on the sample.
- **Vitamin C** at high doses affects some urine tests.
- **Blood thinners** matter for clotting tests and for pressure after the
  draw. We hold the site longer.
- **Steroids** affect glucose and white cell counts.
- **Iron supplements** should be stopped 24 hours before a serum iron or
  ferritin test, not before anything else.

## Before Specific Tests

**Thyroid profile.** If you take levothyroxine, take it *after* the
sample, not before. Morning sample preferred.

**Testosterone, cortisol, prolactin.** Between 7:00 AM and 10:00 AM.
These follow a daily rhythm and an afternoon sample cannot be compared
against the reference range. For prolactin, avoid vigorous exercise and
sexual activity for 24 hours beforehand.

**Urine routine.** Midstream sample. Pass a little into the toilet first,
then collect, then finish into the toilet. The first part of the stream
carries contamination from the skin.

**Urine culture.** The same, but into the sterile container given to you,
and ideally the first urine of the morning. Do not start antibiotics
before the sample is taken — a culture collected after the first dose can
come back falsely negative, and you may be treated for the wrong
organism.

**Stool sample.** Into the container provided, not into a cup from home.
Avoid contamination with urine or toilet water. Bring it in within two
hours, or keep it refrigerated and bring it the same day.

**Glucose tolerance test.** Fast 8 hours. You will have a fasting sample
taken, then drink a glucose solution, then stay in the branch for two
hours before the second sample. You must not eat, drink, smoke or leave
during those two hours — walking around outside changes the result. Bring
something to read, and something to eat for afterwards.

**24-hour urine collection.** You will be given a large container. Empty
your bladder into the toilet when you start, note the time, then collect
every drop for the next 24 hours, including the first one of the next
morning. Keep the container cool. Missing a single sample invalidates the
whole collection.

## After a Blood Draw

Keep the cotton pressed on firmly for two full minutes without lifting it
to look. Lifting it to check is what causes most bruising.

- Do not lift anything heavy with that arm for a couple of hours.
- Leave the plaster on for an hour.
- If you feel light-headed, sit or lie down and tell a member of staff.
  This is common, it is not dangerous, and it passes faster lying down.
- A small bruise is normal and clears in three to five days. A warm
  compress after the first 24 hours helps.

Call us on (503) 555-0142 if the bruise is spreading rather than fading,
if the arm becomes swollen or painful after a day, or if there is
numbness or tingling in the hand. These are uncommon and worth a call.

## After a Glucose Tolerance Test

Eat something as soon as the last sample is taken. Most people feel
light-headed by the end because they have been fasting and then given a
glucose load.

Do not drive immediately if you feel unsteady. Sit in reception for ten
minutes. Nobody is going to rush you out.

## Children

Children can eat and drink normally unless the test specifically requires
fasting. For a fasting test in a young child, book the earliest slot so
the fast is mostly overnight.

Bring a distraction — a phone, a toy, a snack for afterwards. Tell us if
your child is anxious about needles and we will allocate a longer slot
and our most experienced phlebotomist. There is no charge for that.

## Pregnancy

Tell us you are pregnant. It affects reference ranges for several tests,
and it changes which tests we advise against.

Fasting is safe in pregnancy for the periods above, but tell us if you
have gestational diabetes — the fasting advice may differ and your doctor
should decide it, not us.
